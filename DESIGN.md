## Overview

**Creative North Star: "One life. An entire world."**

The established FRAGILE monochrome identity carries the atlas. The work leads and interface controls recede. Animal, Earth and symbols share real triangular geometry.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Black space, white sculpture.
- One locally hosted monospaced type family.
- Spatial discovery through persistent fragments.

## Colors

Foreground white defines models and primary text. Secondary and muted neutrals separate supporting notes without adding an accent color. **The Wire Rule.** All animal, Earth and icon surfaces remain unfilled.

## Typography

IBM Plex Mono uses light display names and regular small controls. Data numerals are tabular. The existing compact mono voice is retained; it is not a new typography direction. Source dialogs use a longer reading measure.

## Layout

Animal mode shows only a centered sculpture, its small common name, and one quiet gesture hint: CLICK TO DISCOVER · DRAG TO ROTATE. The masthead, scientific name, reveal button and navigation are hidden in this chapter. The revealed world has a left rail with the small animal projection, population, habitat and threats; a centered globe; and a right rail with three square terrain studies stacked vertically. Below the 760px breakpoint the chapter scrolls vertically, with population and habitat notes above the globe and conservation pressures below. Information has no fixed sidebar. Desktop chapters fit a single viewport, including short 600px-high displays. A quiet CLICK FOR NEXT ANIMAL action and DRAG TO ROTATE hint sit beneath the species name; no large next button or previous navigation is shown. A small projection of the real animal mesh leads the left information rail and advances to the next species when clicked.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth comes from real perspective and rotating geometry; the regional relief square uses an orthographic projection of sampled elevation data. No decorative shadows, glow, blur or filled/translucent polygons are used. Rear globe lines have reduced opacity so the front coastlines remain legible.

## Shapes

Triangles define actual meshes, fragments and assembled symbols. Habitat pins are solid white octahedra with fine locating rings; sculptures remain unfilled wireframes. Dialogs and collection choices have square corners and thin neutral borders.

## Components

**Journey.** The animal divides only for the display transition; its approved source model stays intact. A deterministic broad cloud reconnects to Earth. During dispersal and reassembly the screen contains only the moving triangular sculpture, with all interface elements hidden. Coastlines, habitat annotations and infographic icons appear only after reassembly is complete. Abstract population and threat icons are omitted; the typography carries those labels. Reduced motion skips the journey.

**Terrain study.** Three squares show the triangular relief mesh, elevation contours and central W–E profile simultaneously, all derived from the same local elevation grid. There is no view selector. Captions identify the representative region, source and amplified relief. Population histories are shown only when dated estimates are documented.

**Controls.** Text buttons have a visible outline on keyboard focus and subdued hover states. Competing journey controls are disabled while moving. Sources and collection use native dialogs with Escape support.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Do preserve the original FRAGILE models and site.
- Do qualify every population figure with its scope and date.
- Do keep habitats schematic and cite the original sources.
- Don't add polygon fill, textures, glow or unrelated colors.
- Don't present an unknown population as zero.


**Forward journey.** Clicking an animal reveals its Earth; clicking the Earth or the small next-species action assembles the following animal. The collection retains direct species selection.

**Spatial continuity.** Desktop chapters share one projection rectangle. The camera reaches its exact destination framing during fragment dispersal, with no further fitting change when the world information appears. Narrow screens adopt the destination layout during the dispersed phase, before the globe finishes assembling. The left information rail distributes its groups over its available height. Recenter sits directly below the globe, separated from the species caption. Population upper bounds use a small Fewer than qualifier rather than mathematical symbols.
